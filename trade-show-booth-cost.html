Budget Planning

"We have a limited exhibition budget." Here's how to plan around it.

Budget is usually the first real constraint in any booth project. Here's a realistic, non-salesy breakdown of what drives trade show booth cost, so you can set expectations before you start designing.

What drives the number

Five things move the price more than anything else.

Booth cost isn't one number — it's several line items that stack up differently depending on your approach. Structure and materials, graphics quality, technology (screens, lighting, AV), booth size, and whether you're renting or buying all shift the total significantly.

Show services — electrical, internet, material handling, drayage, shipping, and installation labor — are separate from the booth itself and are the costs first-time exhibitors most often forget to budget for.

10×10 BOOTH

The most common starting footprint. Costs scale with how custom the structure and graphics are, versus a simpler portable display.

10×20 BOOTH

Enough room for distinct zones. Costs step up accordingly, particularly for custom structure over a modular system.

20×20 AND LARGER

Custom islands and large exhibits carry the widest cost range, driven heavily by technology and finish level.

Renting vs. owning

The math changes with how often you exhibit.

01

One show a year

A rental or portable display is often the more sensible budget decision — you avoid storage costs and a large upfront investment for infrequent use.

02

Multiple shows a year

Owning a custom or modular booth typically becomes more cost-effective after 2-3 shows, since you're no longer paying rental fees repeatedly for the same footprint.

03

Not sure yet

This is exactly the kind of question worth talking through before committing to a design direction — it affects material choices as much as the layout.

Common questions

Trade show booth budgeting, answered simply.

A basic 10×10 setup — a portable display, simple graphics and a table — typically runs a few thousand dollars. A custom-designed 10×10 with more structure, lighting and graphics costs more, but gives you a booth built specifically around your brand rather than a generic display.

Per show, a custom booth usually costs more upfront than a rental. But if you exhibit at multiple shows a year, a custom or modular booth you own and reuse often becomes more cost-effective over 2-3 shows than repeatedly renting. See how custom, modular and portable displays compare.
