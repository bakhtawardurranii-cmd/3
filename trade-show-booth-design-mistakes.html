Booth Not Performing

"Our booth looks outdated and isn't attracting visitors." Here's why.

Most underperforming booths aren't a budget problem — they're a handful of specific, fixable design mistakes. Here's what to check before your next show.

Mistake 01

Message buried in clutter

If a visitor can't tell what you do within a few seconds from the aisle, the layout is working against the message — not just the graphics.

Mistake 02

Blocked or narrow entry

Furniture or displays positioned too close to the aisle can make a booth feel closed off, even when there's plenty of open space inside.

Mistake 03

No clear focal point

Without one dominant visual or product to draw the eye, visitors scan and move on rather than stopping to look closer.

Mistake 04

Flat, uneven lighting

Standard convention hall lighting alone tends to wash a booth out. Accent lighting on a hero graphic or product does more work than most exhibitors expect.

Mistake 05

No obvious place to talk

If there's nowhere for staff and a visitor to have a real conversation without blocking the walkway, interested visitors have nowhere to go.

Mistake 06

Aesthetics without function

A booth built purely for looks, without storage, staff flow, or lead-capture built into the plan, tends to fall apart on setup day.

The real fix

These are layout problems before they're graphics problems.

Almost every mistake above traces back to the same root cause: graphics and furniture were chosen before the layout and visitor flow were planned. Fixing it after the fact means reworking decisions that should have come first.

That's the difference an actual design process makes — starting with custom trade show booth design rather than picking graphics and hoping the layout works itself out.
